Round vs straight: getting the balance right

The room's architecture is all straight lines: walls, doors, the kitchen, the TV wall, the shelf, the shoe cabinet. It looks best with one big shape (the sofa) plus three or four small round accents. That's roughly 30–40% round, and the meter under the 3D view calculates it live as you change options.

Rule 1Only one big curve.If the sofa is curved, make the coffee table square. Two big curves compete and the room looks bloated.
Rule 2Keep the joinery and the rug straight.They're the frame that makes the round pieces look deliberate.
Rule 3Repeat round accents in 3s.For example: ottoman, dining table, mirror. The globe lights count as small accents.
Rule 4Soften edges, don't make everything round.Rounded arms and corners on a straight sofa give warmth without adding another curve.
Combo A (my pick)Straight L-sofa + round ottoman + round dining + round mirror + globe lights.About 37% round. Calm, modern and the easiest to live with.
Combo B (if you love curves)Curved L-sofa + square ottoman + round dining + ribbed-glass dining light.About 41% round. The sofa is the star and everything else steps back.
Combo C (max seating)Full-length straight sofa + round ottoman + 2 pouffes + round dining.About 40% round, and 10–11 seats.
AvoidCurved sofa + round ottoman + pouffes + round table + bubble chandelier.Over 50% round. It looks like a showroom, not a home.

Option 3 · full creative freedom

The wild one: a hotel-lounge hall

I dropped the "sofa + TV unit + dining set" formula and designed around how a long, narrow Indian hall actually gets used: family evenings, guests over, daily meals and tadka from an open kitchen. Built-ins replace loose furniture, so the floor stays open and there's nothing for dust to hide under. Switch to "Option 3" in the 3D view above to walk through it.

The big moves

  • Built-in lounge instead of a sofa. One continuous upholstered seat runs along the intercom wall and wraps under the window, on a sealed walnut plinth with drawers and an LED glow at the base. It seats 7–8 comfortably, is deep enough to nap on, and has no gap underneath to collect dust. The corner pillar is absorbed into the seat back.
  • Big round olive ottoman in the middle, with a walnut tray on top. It works as coffee table, footrest and extra seating, has no sharp corners, and makes it 9–10 seats.
  • A statement glowing globe pendant over the lounge corner, and brass sconces above a big textured artwork. This is the "evening mood" layer.
  • TV on the plain strip beside door 5, set on a walnut panel with a slim console. Door 5 stays a normal door.

Dining + entry

  • The long 6-seater oval is gone. In its place: a round 4.5 ft sintered-stone table on a sculpted walnut base, with 5 tub chairs (6 at a squeeze). Round means no corners in the walkway and more floor around it.
  • A cluster of three glass globe pendants at different heights over the table.
  • Round halo mirror above the shoe cabinet, the same as V4.

This follows 2026 trends that suit your space: banquette and built-in seating, rounded shapes, arches, warm walnut with muted greens, and layered warm lighting (Homes & Gardens, Livingetc, Houzz). The zoning is typical for long, narrow living-dining rooms (The Decor Forum, Apartment Therapy). Rough cost: ₹4.5–6.8 lakh. The built-in lounge (₹1.2–1.8L) is the big item.

Palette: mid-tones that hide dust

Your cream walls stay. Sage, walnut, mushroom and olive all sit well against cream-yellow, and the mid-tones hide Delhi dust between cleans. Pale oatmeal and bouclé look lovely in renders, but here they turn grey within months.

Delhi-proofing

Material choices that stay clean

Sofa fabricTight-weave performance fabric in mushroom or taupe, with zip-off washable covers.Avoid bouclé, velvet, chenille and very light colours. They trap dust and show every mark.
Wall panelsPlain matt, anti-fingerprint sage laminate, with at most a few wide grooves.Deep fluting collects dust in every groove, so I've dropped it from this version.
StorageClosed, handleless shutters (J-profile or push-to-open). No open cubbies.Every open shelf is another surface to dust.
Dining tableMatt sintered stone or quartz top, in a light stone look.Resists haldi and oil stains. Skip glass and gloss, which show dust.
Dining chairsMushroom leatherette or performance fabric, walnut legs.Wipes clean after meals.
RugFlat-weave or low-pile, in a mid-tone with a faint pattern.No shaggy rugs. They hold dust and are hard to vacuum.
CurtainsMachine-washable poly-linen in olive, plus washable sheers, on a ceiling track.Take them down and wash every season.
LightingLED profiles with diffusers, and simple dome pendants.Easy to wipe. Avoid intricate or fabric shades.

Order of work

  1. Measure: the plain wall from door 5 to the window corner (needs about 4.5 ft or more for the TV), the intercom wall from the lobby to the pillar, and the hall width.
  2. Pick the sofa first. Its size decides the rug and ottoman sizes, and where the art and wall lights go.
  3. Electrical: new TV point on the left wall, points for the globe pendant, the two wall lights and the dining pendant, LED drivers, 3000K lights and smart switches.
  4. Carpentry: TV panel and console, the shelf behind the sofa, shoe cabinet and seat, kitchen pillar.
  5. Finishing: touch up the existing cream paint where the carpentry marked it, fit the curtain track, then bring in the furniture, lights, art and plants.
